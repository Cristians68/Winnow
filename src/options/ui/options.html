Saved

Grade feedback

When you tell Winnow a grade is too harsh or too lenient, it saves the grade and which check was carrying it — on this device only. Nothing is uploaded, and nothing here is read by anyone unless you choose to send it. Exporting is how calibration gets fixed; it stays your decision.

What Winnow can reach

Winnow requests one permission, storage, used to remember your settings and the grades described below, plus access to the Amazon storefronts it runs on. It does not request access to your logins, your passwords, your other tabs, or any site that is not Amazon.

You do not have to take our word for it. Open chrome://extensions — or about:addons in Firefox — find Winnow, and read its permissions. Tools in this category vary widely in what they request, including access to authentication information. The permission list is the fastest way to see what any of them can actually reach, and it is worth reading before installing anything that watches you shop, this included.

Grades remembered on this device

When you open a product page, Winnow stores the grade it worked out so the same grade can appear beside that product on search results pages. That record is a list of products you have opened. It stays on this device, it holds at most 500 products for 90 days, and nothing is ever sent anywhere.

Winnow will not grade a product from a search page you have not opened. A search card shows a star average and a rating count and no reviews at all, and the only way to get the reviews would be to load those pages using your Amazon session — which we will not do, because it puts your Amazon account at risk. So a badge that says "not checked" means exactly that, and never a guess.

Developer

Send deep-analysis requests to a server running on this machine instead of the live one. Only localhost and 127.0.0.1 are accepted — a field that took any address would be a way to send page data anywhere, which has no place in a privacy tool. Leave blank for normal use.

What Winnow does with your data

Nothing leaves your browser. All scoring runs locally on the page you are already looking at. Winnow makes no network requests, has no server, and has no account system. The only permission it requests is storage, used to remember the two settings above.

Winnow never crawls Amazon using your session. Amazon's terms prohibit automated data mining, and scraping through a logged-in session puts your account at risk. Winnow only reads what your browser already rendered. This costs some analysis depth, and that is a deliberate trade.

How we make money

Sponsorship, in Winnow's own windows only.

Winnow shows one sponsored message on this page and in the toolbar popup. That is how the work pays for itself. You can switch it off above, and nothing else changes if you do.

The message is fetched from winnow-reviews.vercel.app, which we run. It is a static file, not a third-party ad network, so there is no advertising company in the loop and none to trust.

What the sponsor is told: that a Winnow window opened, and nothing else. The request carries no product, no page address, no search term, no grade and no identifier — there is no field in it that could hold one. Sponsors therefore cannot buy placement against a particular product, because they cannot know which one you are looking at.

What stays true: Winnow still carries no affiliate links and no referral tags, and still takes no payment from any seller, brand, marketplace or advertiser to influence, alter, suppress or promote any grade it produces. Sponsorship never touches the Amazon page and never appears beside a verdict. A tool that earns a commission when you buy cannot credibly tell you not to buy — that is why the money comes from a slot that cannot see what you are buying.

The scoring engine is open source precisely so you can verify no merchant is paying for a better grade.

What Winnow does not claim

Every result is an estimate, not proof. Winnow cannot know that any individual review is fake. It identifies patterns that manipulated reviews tend to produce, and it tells you what each judgement was based on and how confident it is.

Typically only 8–13 reviews are visible on a product page, and those are featured reviews — a biased sample. Winnow shrinks its rating adjustment to account for that, and declines to state an adjusted rating at all when too little of the sample looks trustworthy. "Couldn't read this page" is not a verdict about the product.
