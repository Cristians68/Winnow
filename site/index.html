Why this exists

Mozilla shut Fakespot down on 1 July 2025, saying it "didn't fit a model we could sustain", and shipped no replacement — the announcement pointed people at tab groups and bookmarks. ReviewMeta went dark separately. Millions of people who checked a listing before buying were left with nothing to check it with.

The old model stopped working

Since May 2026 Amazon's review pages return 404 without session cookies, and full review text left the public HTML. A server fetching a listing from outside now sees a fraction of what you see logged in.

So it reads your page instead

Winnow scores the page your browser already rendered. Nothing is crawled and nothing is uploaded — which is why the analysis happens on your device rather than in a data centre.

And never with your session

Winnow makes no automated requests to Amazon, ever. Scraping through a logged-in session puts your account at risk of suspension. It accepts a shallower read instead.

What it checks

Seven signals, each reported with what it found and whether it changed the grade. A verdict you cannot inspect is one you have to take on faith.

  • Rating distribution. Genuine products keep a 1-star tail from damage and defects. A campaign can add 5-star reviews but cannot remove organic 1-star ones.
  • Verified purchases. Unverified reviews are down-weighted, more so at 5 stars.
  • Review language. Disclosed incentives, template-farm boilerplate, and patterns consistent with generated text.
  • Duplicate text. Reviews paraphrased from a shared template.
  • Review timing. Bursts of reviews landing together inside a months-long history.
  • Review substance. Five-star ratings carrying six generic words and no specifics.
  • Community response. Featured reviews months old that nobody ever found helpful.

It tells you when it cannot tell

Amazon usually shows 8–13 reviews on a product page, and those are featured reviews — a small, biased sample. Winnow shrinks its adjustment to account for that, and when too little of the sample is trustworthy it reports no adjusted rating at all rather than inventing a confident number.

Be suspicious of any checker that returns a reassuring score for everything you point it at. That is not detection, it is decoration. "Couldn't read this page" is never a verdict about the product.

Estimates, not proof

Winnow cannot know that any individual review is fake. It identifies patterns that manipulated reviews tend to produce, and always shows what a judgement rests on.

Integrity, not quality

It never reads whether the reviews say the product is good, and never tells you whether to buy. Those are different questions.

What leaves your browser

The grade is computed on your device from the page you already loaded. The reviews, the product and the page address never leave your machine, and there is no account, no analytics and no telemetry of any kind.

Winnow requests one permission — storage — plus the Amazon storefronts it runs on and one host of ours that serves the sponsored message below. It cannot read your logins, your passwords, or any other site. Every extension has to declare this on its own store listing, so don't take our word for it: open chrome://extensions, find Winnow, and read the list. Then do the same for anything else you have installed that watches you shop.

How it's paid for

One sponsored message appears in Winnow's toolbar popup and settings page. It never appears on an Amazon page and never appears beside a grade, and you can switch it off in Settings.

A sponsor is told that a Winnow window opened, and nothing else. The request carries no product, no page address, no search term, no grade and no identifier — there is no field in it that could hold one. So a sponsor cannot buy placement against a particular product, because it cannot know which one you are looking at.

Still true, and binding in the privacy policy: no affiliate links, no referral tags, no merchant relationships, and no payment from any seller, brand or marketplace to influence a grade. A tool that earns a commission when you buy cannot credibly tell you not to buy — which is exactly why the money comes from a slot that cannot see what you are buying.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes, and there is nothing to unlock. Sponsorship covers the work, and it is confined to Winnow's own two windows.

Does it work on my Amazon?

Twenty storefronts, including the US, UK, Canada, Australia, Germany, France, Spain, Italy, Japan, India, Brazil and more. The panel is localised, and when Winnow cannot read a page's language it says so rather than guessing.

Can a seller pay to change their grade?

No. Grades are computed on your device by an engine you can read, before any sponsorship request is made, and no part of a sponsored message reaches the scoring code. That is why the engine being open source matters more than any promise here.

What about Firefox?

A Firefox build exists and is in review at addons.mozilla.org. Firefox asks for site permission separately, so after installing you will need to allow Winnow on amazon.com from the extensions menu.

Why should I trust a tool that grades trust?

You shouldn't, on its word. The scoring engine is open source, the permission list is published by Google rather than by us, and the panel shows its reasoning and its confidence on every verdict. Those are three things you can check.
