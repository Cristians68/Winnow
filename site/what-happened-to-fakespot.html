Shut down 1 July 2025

What happened to Fakespot?

Mozilla closed it. The extensions, the apps and the website all stopped on 1 July 2025, and the Firefox Review Checker built on the same technology had already closed on 10 June 2025. No replacement shipped.

The short version

Mozilla acquired Fakespot in 2023. In 2025 it announced the shutdown on its own blog, and the reason it gave was one sentence: "while the idea resonated, it didn't fit a model we could sustain."

Fakespot was not sold, not open-sourced, and not handed to anyone else. The announcement directed readers toward unrelated Firefox features — tab groups and bookmarks — rather than to any way of continuing to check reviews. ReviewMeta, the other tool people leaned on, went dark separately.

Roughly ten million people had the habit of checking a listing before buying. None of them were given anywhere to go.

Why nothing simply replaced it

It is tempting to assume someone would just rebuild it. Two things made that harder than it looks.

The money. Mozilla is a large organisation and still could not make the economics work. The obvious way to fund a shopping tool is affiliate commission — you get paid when someone buys. That is exactly the wrong incentive for a tool whose job is sometimes to say don't buy this, and several replacements took it anyway.

The data. Since around May 2026, Amazon's /product-reviews/ pages return 404 without session cookies, and full review text left the public HTML. Fakespot's model — you hand over a URL, a server fetches and analyses it — now sees far less than a logged-in shopper does. A server-side checker built the old way is working from a thinner sample than the one already on your screen.

What actually works now

An extension reads the page your browser has already rendered. That is not a preference; it is where the reviews still are. It also means nothing needs to be uploaded and no server needs to crawl anything.

Two things worth checking about any replacement you consider, including this one:

  • Does it take affiliate money? A tool paid a commission when you buy cannot credibly tell you not to buy. At least one widely-recommended Fakespot replacement ships an undisclosed Amazon Associates tag on its "Buy Now" button.
  • What can it read? Every extension must declare this on its own store listing, under Privacy practices. Open the listing before you install, and compare. A review tool that wants to read every site you visit is reading every site you visit.

Where Winnow fits

Winnow was built after the shutdown, for the same job. It reads the reviews already on an Amazon product page and shows the rating the product would have without the ones that look manipulated, with every check shown so you can disagree with it.

It requests one permission — storage — plus the Amazon storefronts it runs on. It carries no affiliate links and won't, as a binding clause in its privacy policy rather than a line in a README. The scoring engine is open source, so a grade you doubt is one you can go and read the reason for.

It also declines to answer when it cannot. Amazon shows only 8–13 featured reviews on a product page — a small, biased sample — so when too little of it is trustworthy, Winnow reports no adjusted rating rather than inventing a confident number.
