Looking for a ReviewMeta alternative?

ReviewMeta's appeal was one number: the rating a product would have once the reviews that failed its tests were set aside. Winnow computes that number too — in your browser, on the page you are already looking at, with every check shown.

Why the old tools stopped working

Both ReviewMeta and Fakespot worked the same way: you pasted a product URL, their servers fetched the reviews, and they returned an analysis. Mozilla shut Fakespot down on 1 July 2025, saying it "didn't fit a model we could sustain". ReviewMeta stopped serving analyses separately.

There is a structural reason the paste-a-URL model is hard to revive. Since May 2026, Amazon's /product-reviews/ pages return 404 without session cookies, and full review text left the public HTML. A server fetching a listing from outside sees far less than a logged-in shopper does — so an analysis built that way is working from a thinner sample than the one on your screen.

An extension reads the page your browser already rendered. That is not a preference for extensions; it is the only place the reviews still are.

The adjusted rating, and when Winnow refuses to give one

Winnow runs seven checks over the reviews on the page — rating distribution, verified purchases, phrasing, duplicated text, posting bursts, review substance, and helpful votes — and reports the star average that remains once the ones that failed are set aside.

It also refuses, out loud, when it should. Amazon typically shows 8–13 reviews on a product page and those are featured reviews, chosen by Amazon — a biased sample, and a small one. Winnow shrinks its adjustment to reflect that, and when too little of the sample is trustworthy it reports no adjusted rating rather than inventing one. "Couldn't read this page" is shown as exactly that, never as a verdict about the seller.

What is different from ReviewMeta

  • No URL to paste. Open a product page and the panel is already there.
  • Nothing is uploaded. The grade is computed on your device. The page you are reading never leaves your browser.
  • The engine is open source. The scoring code is public, so a grade you disagree with is something you can go and read the reason for.
  • No affiliate links. Not now and not later — it is written into the privacy policy as a binding commitment. A tool paid a commission when you buy cannot credibly tell you not to buy.

What it will not tell you

Winnow measures review integrity, not product quality. It never reads whether the reviews say the product is good, and it never recommends buying or not buying. It also cannot see the seller, the price, the shipping origin or the item itself — so the panel includes a short list of things worth checking yourself, which it is honest enough to say it cannot check for you.
